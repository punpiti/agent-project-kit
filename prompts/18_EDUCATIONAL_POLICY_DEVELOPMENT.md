# 18 — Educational Policy Development

Use for university council work, institutional governance, curriculum policy,
education agencies, academic competitions and standards, including work related
to organizations such as university councils, IPST, or IOI governance.

## Route

1. Identify the decision authority, policy owner, affected groups, jurisdiction,
   decision date, and required deliverable.
2. State the problem and intended outcome without assuming the proposed policy
   is the only solution.
3. Separate legal or formal requirements, evidence, institutional constraints,
   stakeholder interests, and value judgments.
4. Map stakeholders: who decides, implements, benefits, bears cost or risk, can
   object, and must be consulted.
5. Compare options, including status quo, using feasibility, cost, capacity,
   equity, legitimacy, enforceability, and unintended consequences.
6. Make claims no stronger than the evidence. Load Research Activities as a
   secondary workflow when external evidence or comparative practice is needed.
7. Specify implementation ownership, resources, timeline, exceptions, review
   mechanism, success indicators, and conditions for revision or withdrawal.
8. Review wording for ambiguity, unnecessary attack surfaces, conflicts of
   interest, procedural fairness, and mismatch between authority and promise.

## Required Review Gate

- What is established by evidence or rule?
- What remains a policy choice or value judgment for the human decision-maker?
- Who has not yet been consulted?
- What implementation capacity has not been verified?
- What external, legal, institutional, or stakeholder feedback is still needed?

Do not treat a polished draft or research summary as proof that the policy is
acceptable, implementable, legitimate, or approved.
